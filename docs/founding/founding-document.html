00 · Summary

At a glance

Musterpass is a QR clock-in and clock-out app for teams. Everyone who arrives scans the poster at the entrance with their phone camera, takes a selfie, passes a one-time location check, answers any forms their company has added, and is clocked in. Managers see who is on site, the hours worked and anything flagged, with a timestamped, photo-backed record ready for payroll, audits or an emergency roll call.

What changed in version 0.4

  • Back to a general clock-in app. Clocking in is the product again; safety is one strong use of it, not the whole product. The six industry templates and the industry step at sign-up are gone.
  • Each company builds its own forms (or none, for a plain time clock), starting from a library of six ready-made forms. See section 01.
  • It's live. Company sign-up and login, a shared Supabase database, server-checked clock-ins and private photo storage run on musterpass.vercel.app.
  • Company branding: each company's logo, colour and welcome line on its clock-in screens and posters.
  • Light and dark mode, and alerts are now red (#CA2A30, white text) instead of pink.
  • A command center for the Musterpass team: every company, a tester bot, and one-click rollback of the live site.
  • A new homepage with an illustrated card carousel, animated how-it-works steps, Log in, and a "Try it out" demo.

For companies

Sign up, add a first site, print its poster. Two starter forms are ready; edit them, add more from the library, or turn them off. One flat price per company, with no per-worker counting.

For workers and visitors

Scan with the phone camera. No app, no account, no paper. Under a minute to clock in, and scan again to clock out.

For managers

Live list of who is clocked in, hours worked, flagged answers and selfies to review, updating by itself. Records and a payroll CSV in one click.

Where we start

Construction and trades in British Columbia stay the first market: sites with walk-on subcontractors, safety forms and emergency musters get the most from proof at the gate. Because forms are built by each company, warehouses, kitchens, cleaning crews and any other team can use Musterpass from day one.

01 · System vision and product overview

Why Musterpass exists

Mission

Make it faster to do safety right than to skip it, and give every company proof that it did.

Vision

No more clipboards at the site trailer. Every worker, subcontractor and visitor checks in on their own phone, confirms the day's hazards and controls, and leaves a record that is complete, signed and photo-backed before they pick up a tool.

The problem

  • Paper safety forms arrive late. FLHAs and sign-in sheets are filled in on clipboards, then collected, scanned or typed up days later, if they are not lost.
  • Paper proves little. After an incident it is hard to show who was on site, when, and whether they were briefed. Signatures can be added after the fact.
  • Liability falls on the company. Missing or late records weaken its position with the regulator, insurers and in court.
  • Nobody knows exactly who is on site. That matters for emergency roll calls (muster) and for paying people correctly.

Values (each one is a product rule)

Proven, not claimed

Every check-in and form carries server time, location and photos that cannot be edited afterwards.

Evidence at the moment, never surveillance

Photos and location are captured only at check-in, check-out and in forms. No tracking during the shift.

Faster than paper

A daily clock-in, forms included, must take about a minute. If paper is faster, we've failed.

Fair to everyone on site

Workers can see their own records. Nothing is changed silently; every correction is logged with who and why.

01 · Photo verification

Three kinds of photo, three jobs

Photos are what make our records stronger than paper. They do three different jobs, and we keep them separate in the product, in storage and in how long we keep them.

1 · Identity

Is this the right person?

  • A selfie taken live in the check-in flow (camera only, no gallery uploads).
  • Shown next to the person's profile photo for a supervisor to review. Mismatches are flagged.
  • Stops buddy punching and borrowed accounts.
  • No automatic face matching for now. That would be biometric processing with stricter legal rules; it is future scope.
2 · Presence

Are they really on site?

  • The phone's location at the moment of check-in, compared with the site geofence on the server.
  • The QR code carries a short-lived token, so a photo of the poster doesn't work from home.
  • The selfie background adds visual proof of the site.
  • Optional per site: a photo of the entrance or posted site board.
3 · Compliance

Is the work set up safely?

  • Photo questions inside the company's forms: PPE worn, harness tie-off, barricades, a clean station, a fridge thermometer.
  • The form decides which answers require a photo.
  • This is the strongest evidence in an audit or after an incident.
  • Kept as long as the safety record itself.

Rules for every photo

  • Captured live in the app with the camera, never picked from the photo gallery.
  • Stamped by the server with time, site, person and location, and stored with a SHA-256 hash so any later change is detectable.
  • Private by default. Viewable only by authorized people in that company, through links that expire in minutes.
  • Separate retention. Identity selfies are kept for a short period (proposal: 90 days, unless attached to an incident); compliance photos are kept with the safety record. Final periods to be set with legal advice.
  • Explained up front. The first check-in shows plain-language consent wording, and we store which version each person accepted.

01 · Forms

Forms each company builds

Every team checks different things, so Musterpass doesn't guess by industry. Each company builds its own clock-in and clock-out forms on the Forms page, or turns them all off for a plain time clock. Forms run in order after the location check; any answer that matches an alert rule is flagged for the supervisor on the dashboard.

Question types

Yes/no · pick one · pick any · short answer · number (with a +/− button, since phone keypads lack a minus) · photo (camera only).

Rules

Any question can be required, and can alert the supervisor on certain answers (for example "No" to "Fit for work?", or a number out of range). A form can ask for a signature.

When

Each form runs at clock-in or at clock-out. Reorder forms, turn them on or off, or delete them. Visitors get their own short form.

The library

End of shiftAny incidents or injuries, anything to reportEveryone (starter form, at clock-out)
PPE checkWhich PPE you're wearing, with a photoConstruction, manufacturing

The FLHA is the short pre-task hazard check used across Canadian construction as part of the hazard assessment that occupational safety rules require.6 7 WorkSafeBC doesn't mandate one form, so the library version is a strong default that each company adapts.

Later, "Tailor my forms with Claude" will write or adapt forms from a description or a photo of an existing paper form. It needs a Claude API key.

The worker's clock-in (every day)

Scan

Phone camera on the site poster. No app.

Who are you?

Name, company and phone once; the phone remembers them after that.

Selfie + location

A live selfie and a one-time location check against the site boundary.

Forms and sign

The company's forms, a signature if asked. "You're clocked in."

Everyone else

Contractors

Scan the same poster and pick "A contractor". The site's company gets the full clock-in, forms and hours. Linking subcontractor companies comes with onboarding (section 09).

Visitors and inspectors

A short visitor form (who you're seeing, purpose, site rules) and a selfie. They stay on the emergency list until they sign out.

01 · A worked example

Northshore Builders

Northshore Builders is a made-up company. It is the demo company on the live site ("Try it out") and the script for the demo video.

Northshore Builders is a general contractor in Burnaby with 60 workers across three sites and about a dozen subcontractor crews. Until now, FLHAs were done on clipboards and collected by the site supervisor at the end of the week.

Setup · Monday, 15 minutes

Priya, the safety manager, signs up, adds the Lonsdale Tower site and prints two posters for the gates. She keeps the Fit for work form, adds Hazard assessment and PPE check from the library, uploads the company logo and invites the supervisors.

Check-in · Tuesday, 6:48 am

Jas, a framer, scans the gate poster, takes a selfie and fills in the hazard assessment: working at heights, controlled by guardrails and tie-off. The form asks for a photo of the harness. Jas signs, and the dashboard shows 23 clocked in.

A walk-on · 7:05 am

An electrician from a subcontractor the company never invited scans the same poster, enters name, company and phone, does the selfie and forms, and appears on the roster like everyone else.

A hazard · 9:30 am

A labourer answers that an excavation has no barricade. The answer is flagged on the dashboard, the supervisor has the area barricaded and countersigns the record.
Three weeks later, a worker is injured on site. Priya exports that day's records in one click: who was clocked in, each signed hazard assessment with timestamps, location and photos, and the supervisor's countersignature. With paper, this would have taken days and might not have existed.

Why now

  • Regulators, insurers and owners increasingly expect documented hazard assessments, and paper is the weak link.
  • Every worker has a smartphone, but most site paperwork is still on clipboards.
  • Certification programs such as COR reward companies with good records, which gives buyers a concrete reason to switch.
  • Existing safety platforms are built for large contractors, with per-user pricing and long rollouts. Small and mid-size contractors are underserved.

06 · Go-to-market strategy

Start on BC sites, spread through subs

  1. Founder-led pilots (months 0–3): 5–10 BC contractors, one site each, free in exchange for feedback and a case study.
  2. Safety consultants and COR advisors: they set up safety programs for many contractors and can recommend us.
  3. Industry associations and events: construction safety associations, trade shows, toolbox-talk content.
  4. Self-serve trial: sign up, print a poster and see the first clock-in within 15 minutes. "Try it out" on the homepage shows the demo without signing up.
  5. Our own demo video: scripted from the Northshore Builders example and recorded from the real app. We study how competitors present themselves, but never copy their videos or materials.
  6. Food service (later): with one restaurant group as a design partner, grow the kitchen forms in the library (temperature logs, opening and closing checks).

Built-in growth

Subcontractors spread it

A sub crew checks in on several general contractors' sites. Each one learns the product and can bring it to its own company.

The poster is the ad

Every gate poster carries the brand and a "get this for your company" link.

Records sell the product

Every clean export shown to an auditor or insurer demonstrates the value to people who influence buying decisions.

Early metrics: time from sign-up to first clock-in, check-ins per site per day, share of check-ins with all required photos, subcontractor share of check-ins, trial-to-paid conversion.

08 · Supabase privacy plan

Built to protect people and evidence

Photos of people plus their location are sensitive personal information under Canadian privacy law (PIPEDA, and BC and Alberta PIPA). These are the technical rules; the legal side (policy wording, retention periods) needs a lawyer before the first pilot.

Where data lives

  • Supabase project in the Canada (Central) region. A project can't change region after it's created, so a project made elsewhere must be recreated before real customers.
  • Separate projects for development, staging and production. Real data only in production.
  • Point-in-time backups on the production project.

Who can see what

  • Row-level security on every table, scoped by company_id and role.
  • Workers see only their own records; supervisors see their sites; safety managers see the company.
  • Automated tests prove company A can never read company B, and that public visitors can't read anything (31 checks pass on the current schema).

Photos

  • Private storage buckets only; no public URLs ever.
  • Photos are uploaded by the server only after the clock-in is validated.
  • The server records time, location and SHA-256 hash; photo rows can't be updated or deleted by users.
  • Viewing uses signed links that expire (two hours today; shorter later).
  • Hidden metadata (EXIF) is stripped from stored copies; the evidence fields live in the database.

Keys and access

  • The secret key lives only in server environment variables on Vercel (marked secret), never in the browser or the repo.
  • The browser uses the publishable key, which can do nothing without RLS permission.
  • Admin accounts require MFA. Staff access to production is limited and logged.

Retention and deletion

  • Each photo stores retain_until. A scheduled job (pg_cron) deletes expired identity selfies.
  • Safety records and compliance photos follow the company's retention setting, with a legal minimum we confirm with counsel.
  • Incident "legal hold" stops deletion for the affected records.

Consent and transparency

  • First check-in shows what is captured, why, and for how long. The accepted wording version is stored.
  • People can see their own records and request access or correction.
  • An append-only audit log records every view of photos and every change to records.
Future face matching (comparing selfies automatically) would be biometric processing. It needs explicit consent, a separate legal review, and is excluded until then.

10 · Development prompt and instructions for Claude

Instructions for Claude

Paste this into a Claude Code session (or keep it as CLAUDE.md) when working on the product.

You are working on Musterpass, a QR clock-in and clock-out SaaS (repo: qr-signin-admin,
fork ANwagha/qr-signin-admin; never target the upstream repo). Read README.md and
docs/founding/founding-document.html first. They are the source of truth.

Context
- Product: scan the site poster -> who are you (once per phone) -> selfie -> location check ->
  the company's own forms (lib/forms.ts; signature only if a form asks) -> clocked in. Scan
  again to clock out. Visitors sign in separately. Demo company: Northshore Builders.
- Stack: Next.js 16 App Router (cacheComponents), React 19, Tailwind 4, TypeScript. Vercel.
  Supabase: Postgres with RLS, Auth, Storage, Realtime (supabase/schema.sql, lib/cloud/).
  Without Supabase keys the app runs browser-only (lib/store.ts); the demo always stays local.
- Work one step at a time (section 09). Commit and push only when asked, straight to main.

Photo rules (never break these)
- Three kinds: identity (selfie), presence (location), compliance (photos in forms).
- Capture live from the camera; never accept gallery uploads for evidence.
- Private storage only, uploaded by the server; view through expiring signed links.
- No automatic face matching.

Privacy and security rules
- Never put secrets in the repo, the browser or a NEXT_PUBLIC_ variable. The secret key is server-only.
- Every table has company_id and RLS; keep the cross-company isolation tests passing.
- Clock-in records can be reviewed and countersigned, never edited.
- Workers don't sign in: the server routes in app/api/clock/ check every request themselves.

Brand rules
- Colors: ink #141414, volt #C6F432, bone #F5F5F0, alert red #CA2A30 (white text),
  graphite #2A2A2A, smoke #8A8A85. Use the Tailwind theme roles, never raw hex in components.
- One volt element per screen. Ink text on volt. 4.5:1 contrast in light and dark mode.
- Big targets for gloved hands, plain words on clock-in screens.

Engineering rules
- Validate everything on the server. Keep request data inside <Suspense>.
- Run `npm run lint` and `npm run build`, and the tester bot, before saying a change is done.
- After UI changes, check the page at 375px and desktop widths, light and dark.
- Use fake demo data and never real people's photos.

When unsure about product decisions, check the founding document. If it doesn't answer, ask.
