00 · Summary

Tock at a glance

Tock is a time clock that lives on the wall as a QR code and in every employee's pocket as their phone. Employees scan the code, Tock checks they're really on-site, and one tap clocks them in or out. Managers see who's in the building right now, and payroll gets hours they can trust.

For owners

Set up a location and print its poster in about 5 minutes. No keycards, no hardware, no installation project.

For employees

Scan with the phone camera and tap once. No app to install, no badge to lose. They can see their own hours.

For managers and payroll

A live headcount, alerts for late arrivals and missed clock-outs, and timesheets ready to export.

Where we start

Small businesses with 10–200 hourly employees on 1–5 sites (cafés and restaurants, retail, small warehouses, clinics, gyms) that use keycards, paper or a wall clock today.

What makes it different

  • Proven on-site. A location check plus a QR code that changes over time, so a photo of the poster doesn't work from home.
  • Three seconds, no install. It runs in the phone's browser.
  • A personality. Bold, quick and a little cheeky, so employees actually like using it.
  • Fair to both sides. Employees see their own hours, and every timesheet edit is recorded.

Status today

A clickable UI prototype with a sign-in screen (still in its original visitor style), an admin dashboard with sample data, and a QR poster generator. Saving data, logins and on-site checks come next (see section 09).

01 · System vision and product overview

Why Tock exists

Mission

Give every small business a time clock its team doesn't hate, and hours its owner can trust.

Vision

Clocking in should take three seconds and feel good. Tock replaces keycard swipes and beige wall-mounted time clocks with a QR code on the wall and the phone already in every employee's pocket. Managers can see who's in the building right now and trust that the hours they pay for were actually worked.

Values

Each value is also a product rule.

Proven, not assumed

Every clock-in is checked on-site by the server. Anything that fails a check is blocked or flagged, never silently accepted.

Location at the moment, never all day

Tock checks where an employee is only when they clock in or out. It never tracks them during a shift or after they leave.

Three seconds or it's broken

The path from scan to "You're in" has no extra screens, forms or loading spinners. If it takes longer, that's a bug.

Fair to both sides

Employees can see their own hours. Timesheet edits never overwrite the original. Each edit records who made it and why.

01 · System vision and product overview

The problem and the product

The problem

  • Keycards and hardware clocks are a hassle. They cost money, get lost, break, and need someone to manage them.
  • Hours are hard to trust. "Buddy punching" (one employee clocking in for another) and padded shifts are common. In one vendor-commissioned survey, 16% of workers admitted to buddy punching, most often adding about 15 minutes.5
  • "Who's in the building?" is hard to answer. That matters for safety (fire roll calls) and for day-to-day operations.

The product

SurfaceWhoWhat it does
Wall QR posterAdmin prints, everyone scansOne per location or entrance. Opens Tock's clock-in screen
Clock-in screen (mobile web)EmployeesRecognizes the employee, checks they're on-site, then one big button: Clock in or Clock out
Admin dashboardManagers and ownersLive headcount, activity feed, timesheets, exceptions (late, missed clock-out), CSV and payroll export
Location managerAdminsCreate locations, set geofences, print QR posters

Core capabilities (final version)

  1. Saved clock-ins. Every clock event is stored and can be audited.
  2. Admin login and role-based access (owner, manager, employee).
  3. Employee identity: employees sign in once on their phone and stay signed in. They never retype their name.
  4. Proof of presence: a geofence check plus a rotating QR code, so a photo of the poster doesn't work from home.
  5. Proximity prompts: "You've arrived at Main office, clock in?" when the phone enters a location (planned for a later phase).
  6. Timesheets and exports: hours per employee per pay period, with exports to payroll.

01 · A worked example

Daybreak Coffee

Daybreak Coffee is a made-up business. It will also be the demo account and the script for demo videos.

Daybreak Coffee runs three cafés with 24 hourly staff. Until now it used key fobs for the back door and a paper sign-in sheet for hours.

Setup · Monday, 8 minutes

Maya, the owner, signs up and adds the Main Street café with a 60 m geofence. She prints its Tock poster and tapes it by the staff door. Then she invites her staff by email.

First shift · Tuesday, 6:52 am

Sam, a barista, scans the poster. Because it's the first time, Tock sends a one-time sign-in link, and Sam stays signed in on that phone from then on. The location check passes (18 m from the café). Sam taps Clock in and sees "You're in at 6:52. Have a good one." Maya's dashboard updates to "7 in the building."

A blocked punch · Tuesday, 7:10 am

Leo is running late and scans a photo of the poster from the bus, 3 km away. Tock shows "Looks like you're not at Main Street. Get closer and try again." The attempt appears on Maya's exceptions list.

A missed clock-out · Tuesday, 3:00 pm

Sam forgets to clock out. At 3:30 Tock sends a reminder and flags the shift. The next morning Maya fixes the clock-out time and adds a reason. The original record and the edit are both kept.
Payday · Friday. Maya reviews the week's timesheets, approves them and exports a CSV for payroll. The whole process takes minutes, not an afternoon of reading paper sheets.

06 · Go-to-market strategy

Start local, then spread

  1. Founder-led pilots (months 0–3): 5–10 local businesses from our own networks. Free in exchange for feedback and a testimonial.
  2. Free tier funnel: self-serve signup, first poster printed in under 5 minutes.
  3. Content and SEO: "replace keycards," "stop buddy punching," "free QR time clock," fire roll-call templates.
  4. Marketplaces and listings: Capterra, G2, GetApp; the Gusto and QuickBooks app stores once integrations exist.
  5. Vertical playbooks: pick one beachhead industry (e.g., cafés/restaurants or small warehouses), build case studies there, then expand.
  6. Partners (later): bookkeepers and payroll providers who recommend Tock to their clients.

Built-in growth

The poster is the ad

Every QR poster says "Clock in with Tock" with a small "get Tock for your team" link. Every customer's staff door advertises Tock to everyone who walks past it.

Employees carry it to their next job

Hourly workers change jobs often. Someone who liked clocking in with Tock is likely to suggest it at their next workplace, sometimes as the manager.

Bookkeepers multiply

One bookkeeper or payroll firm serves dozens of small businesses. If Tock's exports save them time, they'll recommend it to all of their clients.

Early metrics: time to first clock-in after signup, weekly active locations, clock-ins per employee per week, free → paid conversion, retention at 90 days.

Proof of presence

1. Rotating QR token

The QR code includes a signed, time-limited token: a static poster plus a server-checked code, or a rotating code on a cheap tablet display for high-security sites.

2. Geofence

At clock-in, the browser shares the phone's location. The server checks the distance from the location's center against radius_m, allowing for GPS accuracy.

3. Policy per location

Block or allow but flag. Flagged events show up as exceptions for the manager.

Key routes (target)

  • /scan/[locationSlug]: employee clock-in (public URL, requires the employee to be signed in)
  • /admin: dashboard (live headcount, feed, exceptions)
  • /admin/locations: location CRUD, geofence map, poster printing
  • /admin/people: invites and roles · /admin/timesheets: review, approve, export
  • Server actions or route handlers for clock events. All validation happens on the server.

08 · Trust, privacy and security

Built to be believed

These are design rules, decided now because they shape the database and code. The legal side (consent wording, how long data is kept, which laws apply) is a separate decision, listed in section 09.

Clock-ins can be believed

  • The server decides whether a clock-in is valid. The phone only sends evidence (location, QR token).
  • Failed checks are recorded as flagged events. They're never silently dropped or silently accepted.
  • Timesheet edits never overwrite the original record. Each edit stores who made it, when and why.

Each company's data is walled off

  • Every table is scoped by org_id, with row-level security in the database itself, not only in the app's code. A bug in the app still can't show one company's records to another.
  • The app connects to the database with a restricted account that can't bypass those rules.
  • Automated tests prove that company A can never read company B's data, and that an employee can see only their own records.

Employees' location is respected

  • Location is read only at the moment of clocking in or out. There's no background tracking.
  • Leaning towards: storing only the distance from the location and the GPS accuracy, not the exact coordinates. If so, lat and lng would be dropped from clock_events. To be decided along with the privacy decision.
  • The clock-in screen says in plain words what is checked and why.

Reliability

  • Every request carries a reference code that's shown on any error, so a problem can be found in the logs quickly.
  • Errors go to an error tracker (e.g. Sentry). A health-check endpoint reports the database and email status.
  • Tests cover the rules (unit tests), database security (RLS tests) and full journeys in a browser (Playwright).

Operating cost

At pilot scale, Tock runs on the free or entry tiers of Vercel and Supabase. Costs grow mainly with the number of clock events, live dashboard connections (Supabase Realtime) and emails. All three grow with paying customers.

One known limit: Supabase's built-in email service is heavily rate-limited, so sign-in links need a proper email provider (e.g. Resend) before real teams use Tock.

Known technical debt in the prototype

  • Sample data in app/admin/page.tsx; the form in components/SignForm.tsx doesn't save anything.
  • The scan page triggers a Next.js cacheComponents warning (params read outside <Suspense>).
  • The generic "Create Next App" title, indigo theme, and visitor-style form (name + contact) need to become Tock's employee flow.
