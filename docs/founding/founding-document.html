00 · Summary

At a glance

Musterpass is a site check-in platform. Everyone who arrives at a work site scans a QR code, takes a selfie, completes the day's safety form with photo evidence, and signs off. That one scan clocks them in. Managers see who is on site, whether they are cleared to work, and have a timestamped, photo-backed record ready for any audit or incident.

What changed in version 0.3

  • From time clock to site safety. Clocking in is now a by-product of checking in safely, not the product itself.
  • Photo verification in three separate roles: identity, presence and compliance.
  • The first form is the Field Level Hazard Assessment (FLHA), the daily pre-task hazard check used across Canadian construction.
  • A full SaaS journey from company sign-up to daily use, with per-company pricing and a Billing page.
  • Construction first, food service second. Everyone on site checks in: employees, subcontractors and visitors.
  • Supabase and Vercel are now planned in detail, including how photos and personal data are protected.
  • A new name: Musterpass. It replaces the working name "Tock" (see section 07).

For companies

Sign up, choose "Construction", and get a ready-made FLHA, dashboard and site posters. One flat price per company, with no per-worker counting.

For workers and visitors

Scan with the phone camera. No app, no paper. About a minute to check in and sign the day's FLHA.

For safety managers

Live list of who is on site and cleared. Hazards flagged the moment they are reported. Records exported in one click.

Where we start

Construction in British Columbia, then Alberta and the rest of Canada. Food service (commercial kitchens) is the second industry, using the same engine with its own forms.

01 · System vision and product overview

Why Musterpass exists

Mission

Make it faster to do safety right than to skip it, and give every company proof that it did.

Vision

No more clipboards at the site trailer. Every worker, subcontractor and visitor checks in on their own phone, confirms the day's hazards and controls, and leaves a record that is complete, signed and photo-backed before they pick up a tool.

The problem

  • Paper safety forms arrive late. FLHAs and sign-in sheets are filled in on clipboards, then collected, scanned or typed up days later, if they are not lost.
  • Paper proves little. After an incident it is hard to show who was on site, when, and whether they were briefed. Signatures can be added after the fact.
  • Liability falls on the company. Missing or late records weaken its position with the regulator, insurers and in court.
  • Nobody knows exactly who is on site. That matters for emergency roll calls (muster) and for paying people correctly.

Values (each one is a product rule)

Proven, not claimed

Every check-in and form carries server time, location and photos that cannot be edited afterwards.

Evidence at the moment, never surveillance

Photos and location are captured only at check-in, check-out and in forms. No tracking during the shift.

Faster than paper

A daily check-in with FLHA must take about a minute. If paper is faster, we've failed.

Fair to everyone on site

Workers can see their own records. Nothing is changed silently; every correction is logged with who and why.

01 · Photo verification

Three kinds of photo, three jobs

Photos are what make our records stronger than paper. They do three different jobs, and we keep them separate in the product, in storage and in how long we keep them.

1 · Identity

Is this the right person?

  • A selfie taken live in the check-in flow (camera only, no gallery uploads).
  • Shown next to the person's profile photo for a supervisor to review. Mismatches are flagged.
  • Stops buddy punching and borrowed accounts.
  • No automatic face matching for now. That would be biometric processing with stricter legal rules; it is future scope.
2 · Presence

Are they really on site?

  • The phone's location at the moment of check-in, compared with the site geofence on the server.
  • The QR code carries a short-lived token, so a photo of the poster doesn't work from home.
  • The selfie background adds visual proof of the site.
  • Optional per site: a photo of the entrance or posted site board.
3 · Compliance

Is the work set up safely?

  • Photos attached to answers inside the FLHA: PPE worn, harness tie-off, barricades, housekeeping.
  • The form decides which answers require a photo.
  • This is the strongest evidence in an audit or after an incident.
  • Kept as long as the safety record itself.

Rules for every photo

  • Captured live in the app with the camera, never picked from the photo gallery.
  • Stamped by the server with time, site, person and location, and stored with a SHA-256 hash so any later change is detectable.
  • Private by default. Viewable only by authorized people in that company, through links that expire in minutes.
  • Separate retention. Identity selfies are kept for a short period (proposal: 90 days, unless attached to an incident); compliance photos are kept with the safety record. Final periods to be set with legal advice.
  • Explained up front. The first check-in shows plain-language consent wording, and we store which version each person accepted.

01 · The first form template

Field Level Hazard Assessment

The FLHA is a short pre-task check where each worker identifies the hazards of today's work and how they're controlled. It is widely used on Canadian construction sites as part of the hazard assessment that occupational safety rules require, and it must be redone when the task or conditions change.6 7 WorkSafeBC does not mandate one specific FLHA form, so ours is a strong default that each company can adapt.

1 · Job details

Site (from the QR), date and time (automatic), worker and employer company, supervisor, task description, work location on site.

2 · Fit for work

"Are you fit for duty today?" Have you reviewed the site orientation and know the muster point? Any "no" alerts the supervisor.

3 · Hazards (tick all that apply)

Working at heights · falling objects · mobile equipment · excavation or trenching · electrical · hot work · confined space · hazardous materials · noise · weather · slips, trips and housekeeping · manual lifting · other (describe).

4 · Controls

For each hazard ticked: how it's controlled (guardrail, tie-off, spotter, barricade, lockout, ventilation, permit…). Height, excavation and hot-work answers require a compliance photo.

5 · PPE check

Hard hat · hi-vis · safety boots · eye protection · gloves · hearing protection · fall protection. One PPE photo (compliance photo).

6 · Sign-off

Worker signs on screen. Supervisor reviews and countersigns from the dashboard. If conditions change, "Update my FLHA" creates a new version, never an overwrite.
Food service (second industry) uses the same engine with different templates: opening checklist, fridge and cooler temperature log, food-handler hygiene check, and closing checklist.

Export records for audits, COR, insurers or incidents.

The worker's check-in (every day)

Scan

Phone camera on the site poster.

Who are you?

Signed-in workers are recognized. New people enter name, company and phone once.

Selfie + site check

Identity photo and location, checked by the server.

FLHA and sign

Hazards, controls, PPE photo, signature. "You're checked in."

Everyone else

Subcontractors

Scan the same poster. If their company is linked, their records are shared with it; if not, the general contractor still gets the full check-in and FLHA.

Visitors and inspectors

A shorter visitor form (who you're seeing, site rules acknowledged, selfie). They appear on the muster list until they check out.

01 · A worked example

Northshore Builders

Northshore Builders is a made-up company. It will be the demo account and the script for the demo video.

Northshore Builders is a general contractor in Burnaby with 60 workers across three sites and about a dozen subcontractor crews. Until now, FLHAs were done on clipboards and collected by the site supervisor at the end of the week.

Setup · Monday, 15 minutes

Priya, the safety manager, signs up and picks "Construction". The FLHA, dashboard and wording load automatically. She adds the Lonsdale Tower site, prints two posters for the gates and invites the supervisors.

Check-in · Tuesday, 6:48 am

Jas, a framer, scans the gate poster, takes a selfie and fills in the FLHA: working at heights, controlled by guardrails and tie-off. The form asks for a photo of the harness. Jas signs, and the dashboard shows 23 on site.

A walk-on · 7:05 am

An electrician from a subcontractor the company never invited scans the same poster, enters name, company and phone, does the selfie and FLHA, and appears on the roster like everyone else.

A hazard · 9:30 am

A labourer ticks "Excavation" with no barricade photo. The supervisor gets an alert, the area is barricaded, and the FLHA is updated as a new version with the photo.
Three weeks later, a worker is injured on site. Priya exports that day's records in one click: who was on site, each signed FLHA with timestamps, location and photos, and the supervisor's countersignature. With paper, this would have taken days and might not have existed.

Safety is a must-have, not a nice-to-have

Research firms define these markets differently, so we treat the figures as a rough guide.

Why now

  • Regulators, insurers and owners increasingly expect documented hazard assessments, and paper is the weak link.
  • Every worker has a smartphone, but most site paperwork is still on clipboards.
  • Certification programs such as COR reward companies with good records, which gives buyers a concrete reason to switch.
  • Existing safety platforms are built for large contractors, with per-user pricing and long rollouts. Small and mid-size contractors are underserved.

Beyond construction

Food service (second): kitchens run daily checklists and temperature logs on paper for health inspections. Warehouses and public-sector field teams (for example police or municipal crews using site questionnaires) are later options; public-sector buyers will require Canadian data residency, which our Supabase plan already allows.

06 · Go-to-market strategy

Start on BC sites, spread through subs

  1. Founder-led pilots (months 0–3): 5–10 BC contractors, one site each, free in exchange for feedback and a case study.
  2. Safety consultants and COR advisors: they set up safety programs for many contractors and can recommend us.
  3. Industry associations and events: construction safety associations, trade shows, toolbox-talk content.
  4. Self-serve trial: sign up, print a poster and run a first FLHA within 15 minutes.
  5. Our own demo video: scripted from the Northshore Builders example and recorded from the real app. We study how competitors present themselves, but never copy their videos or materials.
  6. Food service (later): once the forms engine is proven, launch restaurant templates with one restaurant group as a design partner.

Built-in growth

Subcontractors spread it

A sub crew checks in on several general contractors' sites. Each one learns the product and can bring it to its own company.

The poster is the ad

Every gate poster carries the brand and a "get this for your company" link.

Records sell the product

Every clean export shown to an auditor or insurer demonstrates the value to people who influence buying decisions.

Early metrics: time from sign-up to first FLHA, check-ins per site per day, share of check-ins with all required photos, subcontractor share of check-ins, trial-to-paid conversion.

08 · Supabase privacy plan

Built to protect people and evidence

Photos of people plus their location are sensitive personal information under Canadian privacy law (PIPEDA, and BC and Alberta PIPA). These are the technical rules; the legal side (policy wording, retention periods) needs a lawyer before the first pilot.

Where data lives

  • Supabase project in the Canada (Central) region.
  • Separate projects for development, staging and production. Real data only in production.
  • Point-in-time backups on the production project.

Who can see what

  • Row-level security on every table, scoped by company_id and role.
  • Workers see only their own records; supervisors see their sites; safety managers see the company.
  • Automated tests prove company A can never read company B, and that public visitors can't read anything.

Photos

  • Private storage buckets only; no public URLs ever.
  • Uploads use one-time signed upload URLs issued by the server after the check-in is validated.
  • The server records time, location and SHA-256 hash; photo rows can't be updated or deleted by users.
  • Viewing uses signed links that expire in a few minutes.
  • Hidden metadata (EXIF) is stripped from stored copies; the evidence fields live in the database.

Keys and access

  • The service-role key lives only in server environment variables on Vercel, never in the browser or the repo.
  • The browser uses the public anon key, which can do nothing without RLS permission.
  • Admin accounts require MFA. Staff access to production is limited and logged.

Retention and deletion

  • Each photo stores retain_until. A scheduled job (pg_cron) deletes expired identity selfies.
  • Safety records and compliance photos follow the company's retention setting, with a legal minimum we confirm with counsel.
  • Incident "legal hold" stops deletion for the affected records.

Consent and transparency

  • First check-in shows what is captured, why, and for how long. The accepted wording version is stored.
  • People can see their own records and request access or correction.
  • An append-only audit log records every view of photos and every change to records.
Future face matching (comparing selfies automatically) would be biometric processing. It needs explicit consent, a separate legal review, and is excluded until then.

08 · Vercel deployment

Ready to ship

Setup

  1. Import the GitHub repository (ANwagha/qr-signin-admin) into Vercel. The framework is detected as Next.js; no extra build settings needed.
  2. Production branch: main. Every other branch and pull request gets its own preview URL to review before merging.
  3. Environment variables, set separately for Preview and Production:
    • NEXT_PUBLIC_SUPABASE_URL, NEXT_PUBLIC_SUPABASE_ANON_KEY (safe for the browser)
    • SUPABASE_SERVICE_ROLE_KEY, STRIPE_SECRET_KEY, STRIPE_WEBHOOK_SECRET, QR_TOKEN_SECRET (server only)
  4. Preview deployments use the staging Supabase project, never production data.
  5. Function region: choose the Vercel region closest to Supabase's Canada (Central) region. If a contract requires that data never leaves Canada, confirm whether that's possible on our Vercel plan before signing.
  6. Custom domain (musterpass.com, once registered) (app.musterpass.com); HTTPS is automatic.

Why it matters now

  • The camera needs HTTPS. Live camera capture in the browser only works on secure (https) pages, so real phone testing of selfies and photos should happen on a Vercel preview URL, not on the local network address.
  • Preview links make it easy to show your boss and pilot customers each step.

Before the first production deploy

  • npm run lint and npm run build pass (they do today)
  • No secrets in the repository; .env.local is git-ignored
  • Remove the developer-only allowedDevOrigins IP (it has no effect in production, but should not drift)
  • Error tracking (e.g. Sentry) and a health-check route
  • Privacy policy and terms pages live

Instructions for Claude

Paste this into a Claude Code session (or keep it as CLAUDE.md) when working on the product.

You are working on Musterpass (formerly the working name Tock), a site check-in
and safety compliance SaaS (repo: qr-signin-admin). Read docs/founding/founding-document.html
first. It is the source of truth for vision, brand, privacy and architecture.

Context
- Product: one QR scan at a site entrance -> identify -> selfie -> site check -> FLHA with
  compliance photos -> sign -> checked in (and clocked in). Construction first, food second.
  Everyone on site checks in: employees, subcontractors and visitors.
- Stack: Next.js 16 App Router, React 19, Tailwind 4, TypeScript. Vercel. Supabase (Canada
  Central): Postgres + Auth + Storage + Realtime, with RLS on every table. Stripe for billing.
- Work one step at a time (section 09), each on its own branch. Don't start later steps unless asked.

Photo rules (never break these)
- Three kinds: identity (selfie), presence (location/QR/site photo), compliance (photos in forms).
- Capture live from the camera; never accept gallery uploads for evidence.
- Store in private buckets only. Server records time, location and SHA-256 hash. View via
  short-lived signed URLs. No automatic face matching.

Privacy and security rules
- Never put secrets in the repo or the browser. Service-role key is server-only.
- Every table has company_id and RLS policies; write tests that prove cross-company isolation.
- Records are append-only: corrections create new versions and audit-log entries.

Brand rules
- Colors: ink #141414, volt #C6F432, bone #F5F5F0, pop #FF5C8A, pop-deep #B8154F,
  graphite #2A2A2A, smoke #8A8A85. Use the Tailwind tokens, never raw hex in components.
- One volt element per screen. Ink text on volt. Never volt on bone.
- Big targets (56px+) for gloved hands, high contrast, plain words on safety screens.

Engineering rules
- Validate everything on the server. Wrap request data in <Suspense> (cacheComponents is on).
- Run `npm run lint` and `npm run build` before saying a change is done.
- After UI changes, check the page in the browser at 375px and desktop widths and show a screenshot.
- Use fake demo data (Northshore Builders) and never real people's photos.

Demo videos
- Script the Northshore Builders flow, automate it with Playwright on a phone-sized viewport,
  record it, and save to docs/demos/. Never copy competitors' videos or materials.

When unsure about product decisions, check the founding document. If it doesn't answer, ask.
